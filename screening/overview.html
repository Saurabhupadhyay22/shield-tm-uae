What screening catches

Every customer, corporate customer, and beneficiary in Shield Astra is screened against a merged catalogue of sanctions, politically-exposed-person (PEP), and adverse-media data. The catalogue is powered by OpenSanctions (an open-source, actively-maintained aggregation of official lists), served locally by a Yente search server that runs inside your deployment.

You don't call OpenSanctions or Yente directly. You ingest a customer through the ingestion API; the continuous-screening worker enqueues that customer; the worker runs the match against every list below; results land as rows in screening_result and update the customer's sanctions_status / pep_status flags. Any rule you write against those flags will fire.

What OpenSanctions covers​

OpenSanctions merges 460+ public datasets into a single catalogue with a common data model. As of the last update shipped with Shield Astra, the catalogue covers:

CategoryCoverageExample sources
Sanctions — internationalEvery major regimeUN Consolidated, EU FSF, OFAC SDN + Non-SDN, UK HMT OFSI, Swiss SECO, Canadian OSFI, Australian DFAT
Sanctions — sectoralSectoral & regime-specificUS SSI, EU sectoral, UK sectoral, Russia-specific sanctions lists
Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)Global heads of state, ministers, legislators, senior judges, senior military, central bank governors, state-enterprise executivesWikidata-sourced PEP graph, extended by curated national lists
Family members and close associates of PEPsFirst-degree relatives + business associates (RCAs), where publicly knownWikidata graph traversal, national PEP registers
Adverse mediaCriminal enforcement lists, wanted lists, most-wanted listsInterpol Red Notices, FBI Most Wanted, national criminal-enforcement registers
DebarmentWorld Bank / regional development bank debarment listsWorld Bank Ineligible Firms, IADB, ADB, AfDB, EBRD debarment lists
Regulatory warningsFSA / SEC / FCA warning listsFCA warning list, SEC PAUSE, ASIC banned list, hundreds more
State-owned enterprisesCompanies majority-owned by sanctioned statesWikidata + curated enrichment

Full source list: opensanctions.org/datasets.

Data refresh: daily by default. Yente re-indexes the catalogue on a cron schedule you set in config/env.production.template (YENTE_UPDATE_CRON).

What Shield Astra does with a match​

The continuous-screening worker matches each ingested customer against the whole catalogue. For every hit it writes one row into screening_result:

FieldMeaning
screening_typeSANCTIONS / PEP / ADVERSE_MEDIA
data_sourceOPENSANCTIONS / UAE_LOCAL_TERROR_LIST / …
matched_entity_nameThe exact entity name on the list
match_score0.00–1.00, from Yente's tokenised match
match_statusHIT / POTENTIAL_HIT / FALSE_POSITIVE / CLEARED
reviewed_by, reviewed_atAnalyst disposition, filled in from Case Manager

At the same time it updates the customer row:

  • sanctions_status → true if any confirmed sanctions hit exists
  • pep_status → true if any confirmed PEP hit exists

Every rule that reads customer.sanctions_status or customer.pep_status picks up the new value on the very next evaluation.

Two UAE-specific lists you load yourself​

OpenSanctions does not (and legally cannot) publish these — they are UAE-authority lists that must be sourced directly:

  • UAE Local Terrorist List — issued by the UAE Executive Office of the Committee for Goods and Materials Subjected to Import and Export Control. Load it as a custom list (Using lists in rules) named uae_local_terrorist_list and screen against it in a scenario rule.
  • UAE High-Risk Corridor List — corridor-level (sender_country → receiver_country) restrictions maintained by CBUAE's Compliance Committee. Load it as uae_high_risk_corridors and reference it in the corridor-risk rules that ship with the Fiat Transaction Monitoring scenario.

Both lists ship as seeded shapes in the starter data — they exist as empty lists in the Lists page on install; your MLRO fills the values and refreshes them per the CBUAE update cadence.

What screening does NOT catch​

Being explicit about the boundary saves time:

  • On-chain wallet risk — mixer exposure, darknet exposure, ransomware exposure. Sourced from Chainalysis KYT, TRM Labs, or Elliptic (a paid subscription you supply). Shield Astra reads those scores off the crypto_transaction row; it does not compute them itself.
  • Real-time transaction-authorisation fraud — card fraud, first-party fraud, account-takeover in the sub-200ms authorisation window. Use a specialised fraud vendor (Feedzai, Featurespace, Sardine). Shield Astra handles AML — structuring, layering, velocity, sanctions — which is measured in seconds to hours.
  • KYC identity verification at onboarding — passport OCR, liveness, biometric matching. Use Sumsub, Onfido, or Jumio. Shield Astra assumes the customer has already been onboarded and just consumes the kyc_status your onboarding system sets.

Licence at a glance​

Third-party dependency — OpenSanctions (commercial licence required for production)

Aggregated sanctions, PEP, and adverse-media data. Powers Continuous Screening and ad-hoc Screening Search.

Licence
CC BY-NC 4.0 (data) — Yente the search server is MIT and always free
Free tier OK for commercial use?
No. The free licence is limited to research, education, or personal use. A commercial licence is required to deploy this in a paying-customer environment.
Approximate paid pricing
~€5–30k per year, depending on volume. Order-of-magnitude only — get a proper quote before budgeting.

The free tier is Creative-Commons-BY-NC — research and non-commercial only. Production deployments serving paying customers need a commercial OpenSanctions licence; it's the single biggest procurement conversation to open with OpenSanctions before go-live.
